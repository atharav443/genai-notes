Where the integral comes from

You can't understand any of the VAE until this step is obvious. So here is the exact reasoning, then a machine to play with.

The reasoning, in six steps

1

We want a model that gives us the probability of a data point, P(x).

2

We assume every x was produced by a hidden code z. So generating x is two steps: pick z, then render x from it.

3

For a specific pair, both must happen: P(x, z) = P(z) · P(x|z).  (how likely the code) × (how likely it renders this x)

4

But we never saw which z was used. Any of them could have produced this x.

5

So we add up every possibility:  P(x) = Σz P(x, z)

6

When z is continuous there is no list to loop over — so the sum becomes an integral:

P(x)  =  ∫ P(x, z) dz An integral is not a different idea. It is the sum in step 5, taken over infinitely many z.
